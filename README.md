### 🧬 About Me
I am a passionate genome explorer, currently pursuing a PhD in Translational Bioinformatics at UMN. My research focus is the dark genome, the vast and understudied regions of DNA once dismissed as "junk." I'm interested in how this dark matter drives aging, age-related diseases, and cellular senescence.

Before this focus took shape, I trained as a registered pharmacist in Nepal, where I worked on several cross-sectional studies and systematic reviews on infectious disease and vaccine acceptance. That path led me to UMN, where I've spent the past two years building bulk, single-cell, ATAC-seq, and spatial genomics pipelines across immunology, cardiology, and developmental biology.

My long-term vision is to turn dark genome and transposable element biology into a real lens for understanding, and eventually intervening in, age-related disease, connecting a part of the genome we've mostly ignored to some of medicine's hardest problems.

I'm drawn to the space between wet-lab biology and computational rigor, building pipelines that turn raw sequencing reads into biological insight. I like working with people who take the genome's dark matter as seriously as its coding 2%.

Let's connect if you're into multi-omics, transposable elements, or the biology of aging and age-related diseases.
